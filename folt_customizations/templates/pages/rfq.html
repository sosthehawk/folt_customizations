THE MARKUP IS OURS, NOT ERPNEXT'S, AND THAT IS THE CHANGE HERE. What this replaced was erpnext's bootstrap column grid with nine inline `style=` attributes and a `.row` nested inside every item cell. Three things were wrong with it for the person it is for -- a supplier, often on a phone, who has been emailed a link and has one job: - the two inputs they are here to fill in were `form-control` fields on a white card, which frappe paints as a borderless grey fill: they read as read-only chips, not boxes to type in; - at the col-sm breakpoint all four columns went full width, so on a phone every number was separated from the word (Qty, Rate, Amount) that gave it meaning; - the sentence saying whether the bid was still open, already sent, or closed -- the one thing that decides whether typing here is worth their time -- was the smallest, faintest text on the page. Everything that is erpnext's business is still erpnext's and still comes from rfq.py: which supplier the visitor is, the currency, the item rows, the already-quoted prices. The one piece of erpnext's own template logic inlined below is the Item Supplier part-number lookup, which came from the `item_name_and_description` macro; it is kept because a supplier's own part number is genuinely useful to them, and inlined because the macro's other half is a nested bootstrap row that cannot live inside a CSS grid cell. The macro's remaining feature, an initials-in-a-grey-box placeholder for items with no image, is dropped deliberately. The class names folt_rfq.js binds to -- rfq-items, rfq-qty, rfq-rate, rfq-amount, tax-grand-total, terms-feedback, folt-save-quotation, and every data-idx -- are unchanged, so the pricing logic is untouched by this. #} {% block header %}
